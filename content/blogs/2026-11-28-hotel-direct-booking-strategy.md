Look at last month's bookings for your hotel. Now count how many of those guests had already stayed with you, or searched your hotel's name on Google, or were in town for a wedding a local family told them about. A good share of them still booked through an OTA, and you paid commission on every one. That is the gap a hotel direct booking strategy closes.

That is the real problem. OTAs like MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, Booking.com and Agoda are useful for reaching people who do not know you yet. But when they also take the guests who were already looking for you, the commission stops being a marketing cost and becomes a tax.

This guide is for owners of independent hotels, resorts and homestays in India who want a practical plan: what to fix first, what it is worth in rupees, and what we would not bother with.

## Quick answer: what a hotel direct booking strategy looks like

A hotel direct booking strategy means getting more guests to book on your own website or phone line instead of an OTA. In practice: a fast website with a real booking engine, your rates showing on Google through free booking links, a reason to book direct (a perk, not just a discount), quick WhatsApp replies, and a plan to bring past guests back.

## What OTA commission is really costing you

Most owners know their commission percentage. Few multiply it out for a full year.

Industry write-ups put OTA commission at [15 to 30 percent of booking value, with most independent hotels paying a base of 15 to 25 percent](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/explainer/4132193/the-independent-hoteliers-playbook-for-reducing-ota-commission) (Hospitality Net, May 2026). Your own rate depends on your contract, whether you pay extra for preferred placement, and how many OTA-funded or hotel-funded discounts you run.

Here is a worked example. The inputs are made up but realistic for a small leisure property.

**Example: a 20-room resort near a weekend getaway town**

| Item | Number |
|---|---|
| Rooms | 20 |
| Average room rate | ₹4,000 |
| Occupancy | 60% |
| Room nights sold in a year | 4,380 |
| Share booked through OTAs | 70% (3,066 nights) |
| Commission rate | 18% |
| **Commission paid in a year** | **₹22,07,520** |

Now say you move just 15 percent of those OTA nights to direct, about 460 room nights. That is ₹3,31,200 of commission you no longer pay.

Direct bookings are not free. Allow about 3 percent for the booking engine and payment gateway (₹55,200 in this example) and ₹10,000 a month for Google and Meta ads (₹1,20,000 a year). You still keep roughly **₹1,56,000 extra** in the year.

One warning from the same maths: if you give a flat 5 percent discount on every direct booking, that costs another ₹92,000 and most of the gain disappears. This is why we prefer perks over discounts, more on that below.

Run this table with your own numbers before you spend a rupee on anything else. It tells you how much a direct booking is worth to you, and so how much you can afford to spend to win one.

## Fix the website and booking engine first

Every other step sends people to your website. If the site is slow on a phone, shows no prices, or ends in "call us for rates", people go back to MakeMyTrip and book there.

The minimum we look for on a hotel website:

- **Live rates and availability** on the homepage, with a date picker that works on mobile.
- **A proper booking engine** that takes payment or a deposit, sends an instant confirmation, and syncs with your channel manager so you never double-book.
- **The full price shown upfront**, including taxes. Since [22 September 2025, GST on hotel rooms below ₹7,500 a day is 5 percent without input tax credit](https://pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=150304) (PIB), which is a lower number than many guests expect, so show it.
- **Real photos of each room type**, not just the lobby and the pool.
- **A clear direct-booking promise** near the booking button, for example "Book direct: free early check-in when available, and we will match any lower price you find".

This is the part most small hotels get wrong, because the website was built once as a brochure and never touched again. If you want help with this, our [website development team](/services/web-development/) builds fast, custom sites for exactly this kind of business.

## Get your official site rate showing on Google

When someone searches your hotel's name on Google, they usually see a price panel listing OTAs. You want your own website in that list.

Google's [free booking links](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/10472393?hl=en) do this. Google says hotel partners pay no fee for these links and there is no cost for clicks. Ranking is based on usefulness to the traveller, not on money.

One important change: Google's help page on [adding rates to your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/10684696?hl=en) says self-service hotel rates ended, and from 30 July 2025 you need a partner (a booking engine, central reservation system or similar) to show rates. So, as of November 2026:

1. Ask your booking engine provider whether they are connected to Google and can switch on free booking links for you.
2. If they are not, that is a strong reason to change providers.
3. Make sure the link Google shows goes to your own booking page, not to an OTA or your Instagram. Google's rules say the booking page must let travellers book directly with you.
4. Check that the price you send to Google includes all taxes and fees, as Google requires.

While you are there, fix the basics on your Google Business Profile: correct phone number, WhatsApp-reachable number in the description, fresh photos, and replies to every review. Our piece on [Google AI Mode ads in India](/blogs/google-ai-mode-ads-india/) explains how Google Search itself is changing, which matters if you also run Google Ads.

## Rate parity: what the CCI order means for you

Many owners believe they are not allowed to sell a room cheaper on their own website than on an OTA. The legal picture changed in 2022.

On 19 October 2022, the Competition Commission of India [penalised MakeMyTrip-Goibibo and OYO](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1869330) (₹223.48 crore and ₹168.88 crore) and directed MakeMyTrip-Goibibo to remove price parity and room availability parity obligations with respect to other OTAs from its hotel agreements, along with exclusivity conditions.

What this means in practice:

- Read your current OTA contract. Do not rely on what a market manager told you on the phone.
- Even where you are free to price lower, a lower headline rate on your site can upset your OTA relationship and hurt your ranking there. Many hotels choose to keep the public rate the same and add value for direct guests instead.
- Value can be breakfast included, a late checkout, a room upgrade when available, airport pickup, or a members-only rate for past guests sent over email or WhatsApp.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your specific contract, speak to a lawyer.

## Win the guests who are already interested

### Answer fast on WhatsApp

Indian guests ask questions before they book. "Is the pool open?", "Can we get an extra mattress?", "Is it safe for kids?". Whoever answers first, with a clear price and a booking link, usually gets the booking.

What we set up for hotel clients:

- **One WhatsApp number** printed everywhere: website, Google profile, Instagram bio, room cards, checkout bill.
- **Quick replies** for the ten questions you get every day, ending with your direct booking link.
- **A reply-time rule**: within 15 minutes during the day. A guest who waits two hours has already booked somewhere else.

### Run Meta and Google ads in short bursts

For paid ads, small hotels should not run broad "visit our resort" campaigns all year. Spend in short bursts around demand you can predict: long weekends, local festivals, wedding season, school holidays, and big events in your city. Use Meta ads to retarget people who visited your website or engaged with your Instagram, and Google Search ads on your own hotel name if OTAs are bidding on it.

### Turn every OTA guest into a direct repeat guest

The cheapest direct booking is a guest who has already stayed. OTAs bring the first stay. Your job is to own the second.

- Collect a phone number and email at check-in for every guest, including OTA bookings.
- Give a small card at checkout: "Next time book direct on WhatsApp and get breakfast free".
- Send one message after the stay asking for a Google review, and one before the next long weekend with a returning-guest rate.
- Keep the list in a simple sheet or CRM, tagged by city and occasion (family, couple, corporate, wedding).

## Mistakes we see, and what we would not do

- **Leaving OTAs entirely.** For most independent hotels that means empty rooms. Reduce dependence, do not cut the cord.
- **Discounting every direct booking.** As the worked example shows, a flat discount can wipe out the saving. Use perks first.
- **Spending on ads before fixing the website.** You will pay for clicks that leave and book on an OTA.
- **Ignoring reviews on Google.** Guests compare your Google rating with what OTAs show.
- **Tracking nothing.** If you cannot see how many bookings came direct each month, you cannot tell what is working.
